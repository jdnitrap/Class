What are the units for force and mass in the American Engineering System?
Force: pounds force (lbf), Mass: pounds mass (lbm).

[Note ID: 2]
What temperature scale is used in the American Engineering System?
Fahrenheit (°F).

[Note ID: 3]
What is the conversion factor for work in the American system?
Work in Btu = (Force in lbf x Distance in ft) / 778.

[Note ID: 4]
What are the main units in the International System (SI/Metric)?
Force: Newtons (N), Mass: kilograms (kg), Temperature: Celsius (°C) or Kelvin (K), Pressure: Pascal (Pa), kPa, or bar.

[Note ID: 5]
What is the freezing point of water in Fahrenheit and Celsius?
Fahrenheit: 32°F, Celsius: 0°C.

[Note ID: 6]
What is the boiling point of water in Fahrenheit and Celsius?
Fahrenheit: 212°F, Celsius: 100°C.

[Note ID: 7]
What is the formula to convert Fahrenheit to Celsius?
°C = (°F - 32) / 1.8.

[Note ID: 8]
What is Absolute Zero in Rankine and Kelvin?
Rankine: 0°R = -459.67°F, Kelvin: 0 K = -273.15°C.

[Note ID: 9]
What is the formula to convert Fahrenheit to Rankine?
°R = °F + 459.67.

[Note ID: 10]
What is the formula to convert Celsius to Kelvin?
K = °C + 273.15.

[Note ID: 11]
Why are absolute temperature scales required in thermodynamics?
All thermodynamic equations require absolute temperature; relative scales produce wrong results.

[Note ID: 12]
What is a system in thermodynamics?
A quantity of matter or space separated from surroundings by a boundary.

[Note ID: 13]
What is an isolated system?
A system with no mass transfer and no energy transfer (theoretical ideal).

[Note ID: 14]
What is a closed system?
A system with no mass transfer, but energy transfer (heat/work) may occur (most common for thermodynamic analysis).

[Note ID: 15]
What is an open system (control volume)?
A system with mass transfer across boundary (inlet/outlet flows) and possible energy transfer (most realistic for practical equipment).
