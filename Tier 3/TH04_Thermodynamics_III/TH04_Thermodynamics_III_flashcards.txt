[Note ID: 1]
What is an adiabatic process?
No heat loss or gain (Q = 0); boundary perfectly insulated; common for turbines/pumps.

[Note ID: 2]
What is an isobaric process?
Constant pressure; boundary moves freely; heat addition/removal at constant pressure (boiler, condenser).

[Note ID: 3]
What is an isentropic process?
No change in entropy (reversible adiabatic); theoretical ideal used for efficiency benchmarks.

[Note ID: 4]
What is isenthalpic process (throttling)?
No change in enthalpy; occurs through valve/orifice; pressure decreases, entropy increases.

[Note ID: 5]
What is an isothermal process?
Constant temperature; system remains in thermal equilibrium.

[Note ID: 6]
What are the three requirements for steady-flow conditions?
(1) Constant mass flow rate (m_in = m_out), (2) Constant fluid properties at each point, (3) Constant rates of heat/work.

[Note ID: 7]
What is the mechanical equivalent of heat?
1 Btu = 778 ft-lbf or 1055 Joules.

[Note ID: 8]
What does the Second Law of Thermodynamics state?
No engine can convert all heat to work; heat rejection always required; maximum efficiency depends only on temperatures.

[Note ID: 9]
What is Carnot efficiency formula?
η_Carnot = (T_hot - T_cold) / T_hot = 1 - (T_cold / T_hot).

[Note ID: 10]
What is entropy change formula?
ΔS = Q / T (units: Btu/lbm-R); more heat at low temperature creates larger entropy change.

[Note ID: 11]
What is the steady-flow energy equation for turbines?
W_turbine = h_in - h_out (enthalpy drop equals work output).

[Note ID: 12]
What is the steady-flow energy equation for pumps?
W_pump = h_out - h_in (work input equals enthalpy rise).

[Note ID: 13]
What does a steam generator (boiler) do?
Raises feedwater enthalpy to produce steam through isobaric heat addition.

[Note ID: 14]
What does a condenser do?
Removes latent heat of condensation; collapses steam volume; maintains vacuum; improves turbine efficiency.

[Note ID: 15]
Why is condensate subcooling important?
Prevents pump cavitation; optimal: 10-20°F below saturation.

[Note ID: 16]
What is nozzle function?
Converts pressure/enthalpy to velocity/kinetic energy through shaped passages.

[Note ID: 17]
What are convergent and divergent nozzles?
Convergent: area decreases, velocity increases, pressure decreases. Divergent: area increases, velocity decreases, pressure increases.

[Note ID: 18]
What does turbine efficiency compare?
Ratio of actual work to ideal work; typically 80-90%.

[Note ID: 19]
What does pump efficiency compare?
Ratio of ideal work to actual work (opposite of turbine!); typically 75-85%.

[Note ID: 20]
What is net cycle work?
W_net = W_turbine - W_pump; pump work is only 1-5% of turbine work.
