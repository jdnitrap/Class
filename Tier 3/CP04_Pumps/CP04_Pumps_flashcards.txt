What is the fundamental function of a pump?
To move a given volume of fluid at a specific pressure from one level to another while overcoming all flow resistance.

[Note ID: 2]
What are the two main pump categories?
Kinetic (dynamic) pumps and Positive displacement pumps.

[Note ID: 3]
How do Kinetic (dynamic) pumps operate?
By converting mechanical energy into kinetic energy (velocity), then converting velocity into pressure energy.

[Note ID: 4]
How do Positive displacement pumps operate?
By trapping a fixed volume of fluid and forcing it into the discharge line repeatedly.

[Note ID: 5]
What type of pump is best for moving large volumes at moderate to low pressures?
Centrifugal (kinetic) pumps - the most common in plant applications.

[Note ID: 6]
What types of pumps are positive displacement pumps?
Gear pumps, screw pumps, piston pumps, and diaphragm pumps.

[Note ID: 7]
When are positive displacement pumps preferred?
For moving small volumes at high pressures, or for moving viscous fluids.

[Note ID: 8]
What is pump capacity?
The amount of fluid a pump moves, normally expressed in gallons per minute (gpm) or cubic feet per second (cfs).

[Note ID: 9]
What is static head?
A difference in fluid levels (elevation head), normally stated in feet of water column.

[Note ID: 10]
What does static head represent?
The pressure energy needed to lift fluid vertically against gravity without accounting for friction or flow velocity.

[Note ID: 11]
What is dynamic head?
Energy required to set fluid in motion and overcome resistance, including velocity head and friction head.

[Note ID: 12]
What is total head?
The sum of static head and dynamic head for a given operating condition.

[Note ID: 13]
What is total suction head (Hs)?
The amount of pressure available at pump suction, expressed in feet of water column.

[Note ID: 14]
What is a positive suction head?
When the suction source fluid level is above the pump centerline.

[Note ID: 15]
What is suction lift?
A negative suction head that occurs when the suction source is below the pump centerline and the pump must "lift" the fluid.

[Note ID: 16]
What is total discharge head (Ht)?
Sum of static discharge head and dynamic head of piping downstream of the pump.

[Note ID: 17]
What is total head also called?
Total developed head or total dynamic head (TDH) - the pressure rise the pump must produce.

[Note ID: 18]
What is Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH)?
The absolute pressure at pump suction minus the vapor pressure of the fluid at that point.

[Note ID: 19]
What happens when NPSH reaches zero?
A partial vacuum forms and fluid begins to flash (boil) into vapor, risking cavitation.

[Note ID: 20]
What is cavitation?
Formation of vapor bubbles when pump suction pressure approaches saturation pressure, causing damage to impeller and equipment.

[Note ID: 21]
What causes the distinctive noise during cavitation?
The vapor bubbles collapsing or imploding under high pressure.

[Note ID: 22]
What damage does cavitation cause to the impeller?
Pitting and erosion of the impeller surface, reducing efficiency and creating imbalance.

[Note ID: 23]
What are indicators of cavitation?
Distinctive noise (sounds like pumping rocks), vibration, motor current fluctuations, and fluctuations in discharge pressure/flow.

[Note ID: 24]
What is the primary method to prevent cavitation during startup?
Vent all air from suction lines and fill them completely with fluid before starting.

[Note ID: 25]
How can cavitation be prevented during operation?
Maintain adequate NPSH by raising absolute pressure above saturation pressure, keeping system temperature low, and minimizing suction line restrictions.

[Note ID: 26]
What does a pump operational curve show?
The relationship between flow rate and the head (pressure) a pump can develop at different operating points.

[Note ID: 27]
Where does a pump's operating point occur?
Where the pump curve intersects the system resistance curve (system head curve).

[Note ID: 28]
How does pump efficiency vary?
Decreases as flow rate deviates from the pump's design point; peak efficiency occurs in specific flow/head ranges.

[Note ID: 29]
What effects does operating outside the design point have?
Wastes energy as heat, accelerates wear, and can cause cavitation.

[Note ID: 30]
What temperature effects impact pump performance?
Higher temperature decreases viscosity and increases saturation pressure, both negatively affecting pump operation and NPSH.
