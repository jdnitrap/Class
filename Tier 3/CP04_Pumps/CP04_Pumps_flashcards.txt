[Note ID: 1]
What is the fundamental function of a pump?
Move fluid volume at specific pressure between levels while overcoming flow resistance.

[Note ID: 2]
What are the two main pump categories?
Kinetic (dynamic) and Positive displacement.

[Note ID: 3]
How do Kinetic pumps operate?
Convert mechanical energy → kinetic energy (velocity) → pressure energy.

[Note ID: 4]
How do Positive displacement pumps operate?
Trap fixed fluid volume and force it repeatedly into discharge line.

[Note ID: 5]
What pump type is best for large volumes at moderate/low pressures?
Centrifugal (kinetic) pumps. Most common in plants.

[Note ID: 6]
What types are positive displacement pumps?
Gear, screw, piston, diaphragm pumps.

[Note ID: 7]
When are positive displacement pumps preferred?
Small volumes at high pressures, or viscous fluids.

[Note ID: 8]
What is pump capacity?
Amount of fluid moved. Units: gpm (gallons/min) or cfs (cubic feet/sec).

[Note ID: 9]
What is static head?
Difference in fluid levels (elevation head). Stated in feet of water column.

[Note ID: 10]
What does static head represent?
Pressure energy to lift fluid vertically against gravity (no friction/flow).

[Note ID: 11]
What is dynamic head?
Energy to set fluid in motion and overcome resistance (velocity + friction heads).

[Note ID: 12]
What is total head?
Static head + Dynamic head for given condition.

[Note ID: 13]
What is total suction head (Hs)?
Pressure available at pump suction. Expressed in feet of water column.

[Note ID: 14]
What is positive suction head?
Suction source fluid level above pump centerline.

[Note ID: 15]
What is suction lift?
Negative suction head. Suction source below pump centerline. Pump must "lift" fluid.

[Note ID: 16]
What is total discharge head (Ht)?
Static discharge head + dynamic head downstream of pump.

[Note ID: 17]
What is total head also called?
Total developed head (TDH). Pressure rise pump must produce.

[Note ID: 18]
What is Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH)?
Absolute suction pressure minus fluid saturation vapor pressure.

[Note ID: 19]
What happens when NPSH reaches zero?
Partial vacuum forms. Fluid flashes into vapor. Cavitation risk.

[Note ID: 20]
What is cavitation?
Vapor bubbles form when suction pressure approaches saturation. Damages impeller/equipment.

[Note ID: 21]
What causes cavitation noise?
Vapor bubbles collapsing/imploding under high pressure.

[Note ID: 22]
What damage does cavitation cause?
Impeller pitting/erosion. Reduces efficiency. Creates imbalance.

[Note ID: 23]
What are cavitation indicators?
Distinctive noise (rocks), vibration, motor current fluctuations, discharge pressure/flow fluctuations.

[Note ID: 24]
Primary cavitation prevention during startup?
Vent all air from suction lines. Fill completely with fluid before starting.

[Note ID: 25]
How prevent cavitation during operation?
Maintain adequate NPSH. Raise absolute suction pressure. Keep temp low. Minimize suction restrictions.

[Note ID: 26]
What does pump operational curve show?
Relationship between flow rate and head (pressure) pump develops at different points.

[Note ID: 27]
Where does pump operating point occur?
Intersection of pump curve and system resistance curve (system head curve).

[Note ID: 28]
How does pump efficiency vary?
Decreases as flow deviates from design point. Peak efficiency at specific flow/head.

[Note ID: 29]
Effects of operating outside design point?
Wastes energy as heat. Accelerates wear. Can cause cavitation.

[Note ID: 30]
What temperature effects impact pump performance?
Higher temp: viscosity ↓, saturation pressure ↑. Both hurt pump operation and NPSH.
