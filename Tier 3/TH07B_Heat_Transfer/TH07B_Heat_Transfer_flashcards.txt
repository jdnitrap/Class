What are the three basic modes of heat transfer?
Conduction (molecular interactions), Convection (bulk fluid motion), Radiation (electromagnetic waves).

[Note ID: 2]
Which heat transfer modes dominate in PWR plants?
Conduction and convection; radiation is negligible during normal operation but significant in accident conditions.

[Note ID: 3]
What is specific heat capacity (cp) and its formula?
Heat required to raise one unit mass by one degree; Q = m × cp × ΔT; 1 Btu raises 1 lbm of water by 1°F.

[Note ID: 4]
What is conduction equation and what factors affect it?
Q = kAΔT / x; higher k or A increases rate; higher x decreases rate; metals conduct well; gases/ceramics conduct poorly.

[Note ID: 5]
Which materials have high thermal conductivity (k)?
Copper (133.0), Iron (39.0), 90-10 CuNi (23) Btu/hr-ft-°F; metals conduct heat well.

[Note ID: 6]
Which materials have low thermal conductivity?
Air (0.0145), Rock Wool (0.03), Concrete (0.2-0.7) Btu/hr-ft-°F; gases and insulation conduct poorly.

[Note ID: 7]
What is convection?
Heat transfer between fluid and surface via circulation/mixing; boundary layer is thin film next to surface.

[Note ID: 8]
What is natural convection versus forced convection?
Natural: density-driven. Forced: pump-driven (more efficient, easier control); virtually all plant convection is forced.

[Note ID: 9]
What is boundary layer thickness and what affects it?
Thin stagnant film next to surface; affected by: temperature (higher T thins layer), flow rate (higher flow thins layer), surface roughness.

[Note ID: 10]
What is heat transfer coefficient (hf) in convection?
hf = kf / xf; where kf is boundary layer conductivity and xf is thickness; higher hf means better heat transfer.

[Note ID: 11]
What are the three layers in heat exchanger transfer?
(1) Convection hot fluid to tube, (2) Conduction through tube wall, (3) Convection tube to cold fluid (all in series).

[Note ID: 12]
What does overall heat transfer coefficient (Uo) represent?
Uo = 1 / total resistance (sum of all three resistances); Q = Uo × Ao × ΔTo.

[Note ID: 13]
How does fouling affect overall heat transfer coefficient?
Fouling adds extra layer; 2 mil scale can drop Uo ~30%; monitored to detect degradation early.

[Note ID: 14]
What is radiation heat transfer?
Transfers heat via electromagnetic waves (no medium needed); Stefan-Boltzmann: Q = σ × A × ε × (T⁴).

[Note ID: 15]
Why is radiation negligible in normal PWR operation?
Requires extremely high temperature difference due to T⁴ relationship; not workable for normal operation but critical in accidents.

[Note ID: 16]
What is single-phase heat transfer?
Both fluids remain liquid or gas; equations: Q = m × cp × ΔT.

[Note ID: 17]
What is two-phase heat transfer?
One fluid changes phase (condenser, steam generator); equations: Q = m × Δh (uses enthalpy change).

[Note ID: 18]
What does CTP represent?
Core Thermal Power; heat input rate from core to Reactor Coolant System.

[Note ID: 19]
What are the two methods to calculate CTP?
RCS heat balance: Q = m × cp × ΔT. Secondary heat balance: Q = m × Δh (steam - feedwater).

[Note ID: 20]
What is condensate depression and optimal range?
Tsat - Tcondensate; prevents pump cavitation; optimal: 10-20°F; excessive wastes heat and increases corrosion.
