What are the five essential elements of a thermodynamic cycle?
(1) Working fluid (steam/gas), (2) Engine (turbine/piston), (3) Heat source (boiler), (4) Heat sink (condenser), (5) Device to move working fluid (pump/compressor).

[Note ID: 2]
What is cycle efficiency?
Cycle efficiency = (Q_added - Q_rejected) / Q_added = net work out / heat added.

[Note ID: 3]
What is the Carnot cycle?
Theoretical maximum efficiency cycle using four processes: isothermal expansion, adiabatic expansion, isothermal compression, adiabatic compression.

[Note ID: 4]
What is Carnot efficiency dependent on?
Only the absolute temperatures of heat source and sink (K); independent of working fluid type.

[Note ID: 5]
How does isothermal expansion occur in Carnot cycle?
Temperature constant; heat absorbed from heat source; pressure decreases, volume increases; system produces work.

[Note ID: 6]
How does adiabatic expansion occur in Carnot cycle?
No heat transfer; temperature and pressure decrease; working fluid expands, extracting maximum work.

[Note ID: 7]
What is the Rankine cycle?
More realistic steam cycle used in actual power plants; operates with four main processes.

[Note ID: 8]
What are the four processes in the Rankine cycle?
(1) Pump compression (adiabatic), (2) Heat addition (isobaric in boiler), (3) Turbine expansion (adiabatic), (4) Heat rejection (isobaric in condenser).

[Note ID: 9]
How is pump compression in Rankine cycle?
Adiabatic; increases liquid pressure; requires work input.

[Note ID: 10]
How is heat addition in Rankine cycle?
Constant-pressure; boiler raises feedwater enthalpy; converts liquid to saturated or superheated steam.

[Note ID: 11]
How is turbine expansion in Rankine cycle?
Adiabatic; decreases pressure and enthalpy; produces useful work output.

[Note ID: 12]
How is heat rejection in Rankine cycle?
Constant-pressure; condenser removes latent heat of condensation; converts exhausted steam to liquid.

[Note ID: 13]
What is net work in Rankine cycle?
W_net = W_turbine - W_pump; pump work typically 1-5% of turbine work.

[Note ID: 14]
How does Rankine cycle compare to Carnot?
More realistic (uses isobars instead of isotherms); easier to analyze using steam tables; lower efficiency but achievable in practice.

[Note ID: 15]
What is the primary method to increase heat source temperature?
Superheat steam in boiler; increases energy input; larger temperature difference with heat sink; limited by material strength.

[Note ID: 16]
What is the primary method to decrease heat sink temperature?
Improve condenser cooling; deep vacuum in condenser maximizes efficiency gain; limited by ambient temperature.

[Note ID: 17]
How do irreversibilities affect Rankine cycle efficiency?
Real turbines and pumps add entropy from friction losses, reducing efficiency compared to ideal cycle.

[Note ID: 18]
What is the effect of pump work on Rankine cycle?
Pump requires 1-5% of turbine output; net work = turbine work minus pump work.

[Note ID: 19]
How does condenser vacuum improve efficiency?
Lower condenser pressure/temperature increases temperature difference between source and sink, improving Carnot efficiency.

[Note ID: 20]
What working fluid is used in Rankine cycle for power plants?
Steam (water); chosen for abundance, properties, and ability to absorb/reject large quantities of heat.
