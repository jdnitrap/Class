# TH02_Thermodynamics_I - Exam Questions

## Question ID: 31
### Question: The First Law of Thermodynamics states that energy:

**Options:**
- a) Always decreases in any process
- b) Cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another
- c) Can only flow from hot to cold
- d) Is lost as heat in all processes


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** The First Law (Energy Conservation): Energy entering a system equals energy stored in or leaving it. E in = E stored + E out.

## Question ID: 32
### Question: Heat and work are best classified as which type of energy?

**Options:**
- a) Stored energy forms
- b) Transient (energy in transit) forms of energy
- c) Potential energy
- d) Kinetic energy


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Heat and work are transient energy forms—energy in transit. They exist only during energy transfer between systems and surroundings.

## Question ID: 33
### Question: Which equation correctly defines enthalpy (H)?

**Options:**
- a) H = U - PV
- b) H = U + PV
- c) H = U/PV
- d) H = PV - U


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Enthalpy H = U + PV, where U is internal energy and PV is flow energy. This is a fundamental thermodynamic property.

## Question ID: 34
### Question: What is the term for energy a substance possesses due to its position or height relative to a reference point?

**Options:**
- a) Kinetic Energy
- b) Internal Energy
- c) Potential Energy
- d) Flow Energy


**Correct:** c
**Explanation:** Potential Energy (PE) depends on position. PE = mzg/gc, where z is height above a reference point.

## Question ID: 36
### Question: Internal energy is best described as which of the following?

**Options:**
- a) Energy from chemical reactions
- b) The sum of the microscopic energy forms (molecular rotation, vibration, and translation) within a substance
- c) Energy stored in compressed springs
- d) Energy from electrical sources


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Internal energy (U) is the sum of all microscopic forms: molecular rotation, vibration, and translational kinetic energy.

- a) h = u - Pv
- b) h = u + Pv

## Question ID: 38
### Question: For a closed system with no change in potential or kinetic energy, the General Energy Equation reduces to which relationship?

**Options:**
- a) Q = W
- b) ΔU = Q + W
- c) Change in enthalpy = heat transferred to the system minus work done by the system (Δh = q - w)
- d) Q = U + PV


**Correct:** c
**Explanation:** For closed systems with no PE/KE change: Δh = q - w. This is the simplified First Law energy equation.

## Question ID: 87
### Question: Flow work (PV energy) is the energy a fluid possesses due to:

**Options:**
- a) Its internal molecular motion
- b) Its position above a reference point
- c) Its pressure and volume (the work required to push the fluid into a system)
- d) Its velocity


**Correct:** c
**Explanation:** Flow energy (PV) is the work required to push a fluid of volume V at pressure P into a system. It equals P×v per unit mass.

## Question ID: 88
### Question: In thermodynamic sign conventions, when heat is removed from a system, Q is considered:

**Options:**
- a) Positive
- b) Negative
- c) Zero
- d) Undefined


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Sign convention: Q added (into system) = positive; Q removed (out of system) = negative. Similarly, W done by system = positive, W done on system = negative.

---

## Question ID: 89
### Question: When work is done ON a system (compression), the work value is considered:

**Options:**
- a) Positive
- b) Negative
- c) Zero
- d) Depends on the gas type


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Sign convention: Work done BY the system (expansion, turbine) = positive. Work done ON the system (compression, pump) = negative.

- b) A measure of the unavailable energy in a fluid at a given state; increases with irreversibility
- c) The rate of heat transfer

## Question ID: 92
### Question: In a steady flow process, what remains constant by definition?

**Options:**
- a) Temperature throughout the system
- b) The mass flow rate in equals mass flow rate out, and working-fluid properties are constant at any location
- c) All pressures in the system
- d) All velocities in the system


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Steady flow: constant mass flow rate in/out, constant properties at each location over time, constant heat/work transfer rates. Most power equipment operates in steady flow.
