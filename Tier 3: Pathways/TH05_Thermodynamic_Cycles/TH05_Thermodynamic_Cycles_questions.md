## Question ID: 62
### Question: What are the five essential elements of a thermodynamic cycle?

**Options:**
- a) A working fluid, an engine to convert heat to work, a heat source, a heat sink, and a device to move the working fluid
- b) A compressor, turbine, pump, condenser, and boiler
- c) Temperature, pressure, volume, enthalpy, and entropy
- d) Input, output, efficiency, work, and heat

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** a

**Explanation:** The five essential elements of a thermodynamic cycle are: a working fluid, an engine to convert heat to work, a heat source, a heat sink, and a device to move the working fluid.

## Question ID: 63
### Question: The Carnot cycle consists of which four processes?

**Options:**
- a) Two isothermal processes and two adiabatic (isentropic) processes
- b) Two isochoric processes and two isobaric processes
- c) Compression, heating, expansion, and cooling
- d) Four isenthalpic processes

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** a

**Explanation:** The Carnot cycle consists of two isothermal processes and two adiabatic (isentropic) processes, operating between two heat reservoirs.

**Options:**

- b) Increase the amount of working fluid and reduce system pressure
- c) Use a more efficient turbine and condenser

## Question ID: 68
### Question: How does increasing condenser vacuum (lowering absolute condenser pressure) affect cycle efficiency?

**Options:**
- a) It increases efficiency, because more work is extracted from the steam and less heat is rejected
- b) It decreases efficiency due to higher pumping losses
- c) It has no effect on efficiency
- d) It increases efficiency only if feedwater heating is also used

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** a

**Explanation:** Lower condenser pressure increases the expansion ratio in the turbine, extracting more work per unit of steam and improving efficiency.

## Question ID: 106
### Question: What is the correct formula for Carnot cycle efficiency when using absolute temperature?

**Options:**
- a) η = 1 - (T_hot/T_cold)
- b) η = 1 - (T_cold/T_hot)
- c) η = (T_hot - T_cold)/T_cold
- d) η = T_hot/(T_hot + T_cold)

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** b

**Explanation:** Carnot efficiency η = 1 - (T_cold/T_hot) where temperatures are absolute (R or K). Higher source temperature or lower sink temperature increases efficiency.

## Question ID: 107
### Question: In the Rankine cycle, which process has constant pressure in the boiler?

**Options:**
- a) Isentropic pump compression
- b) Constant-pressure heat addition to produce steam
- c) Isentropic turbine expansion
- d) Constant-pressure heat rejection in condenser

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** b

**Explanation:** The Rankine cycle boiler process is isobaric (constant pressure). Heat addition occurs at constant system pressure to generate steam.

## Question ID: 108
### Question: Which of the following factors does NOT typically improve cycle efficiency?

**Options:**
- a) Increasing steam temperature in the boiler
- b) Decreasing condenser pressure (raising vacuum)
- c) Increasing steam flow rate to the turbine
- d) Using feedwater heating

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** c

**Explanation:** Increasing steam flow rate increases power output but does not improve efficiency. Efficiency (%) is determined by cycle temperatures/pressures and losses.

## Question ID: 109
### Question: Why is a higher steam quality important in power plant turbines?

**Options:**
- a) To increase condenser temperature
- b) To protect turbine blades from moisture erosion damage and improve steam expansion efficiency
- c) To reduce the condenser cooling water requirement
- d) To decrease turbine work output

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** b

**Explanation:** Wet steam (low quality) damages turbine blades through moisture impingement. High quality steam (>99%) ensures protection and optimal expansion characteristics.
