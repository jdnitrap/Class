# TH05_Thermodynamic_Cycles - Quick Reference Flashcards

## Flashcard ID: 1
### Question: What are the five essential elements of a thermodynamic cycle?

1) Working fluid (steam or gas), 2) Engine (turbine or piston) to convert heat to work, 3) Heat source (reactor/boiler), 4) Heat sink (condenser/cooling), 5) Device to move fluid (pump/compressor).

---

## Flashcard ID: 2
### Question: What is the difference between a thermodynamic process and a thermodynamic cycle?

A process is a change of state in one direction. A cycle is a series of processes that returns the working fluid to its initial state, forming a closed loop used for continuous energy conversion.

---

## Flashcard ID: 3
### Question: How is thermodynamic cycle efficiency defined?

Cycle efficiency (η) = Net work produced / Heat supplied = (Q_added - Q_rejected) / Q_added. Expressed as a percentage, it represents the portion of heat energy converted to useful work.

---

## Flashcard ID: 4
### Question: Describe the Carnot cycle and why it matters to power plant design.

The Carnot cycle is an ideal cycle with the highest possible thermal efficiency for an engine operating between two constant temperature reservoirs. It consists of two isothermal processes and two adiabatic processes. The Carnot efficiency sets the theoretical maximum that real cycles can approach but never reach.

---

## Flashcard ID: 5
### Question: Calculate the Carnot efficiency when a heat engine receives heat at 540°F and rejects heat at 60°F.

Convert to absolute temperature (Rankine): T_hot = 540 + 460 = 1000°R, T_cold = 60 + 460 = 520°R. η = (T_hot - T_cold) / T_hot = (1000 - 520) / 1000 = 480 / 1000 = 48.0%.

---

## Flashcard ID: 6
### Question: How does the Second Law of Thermodynamics impact power plant design and operation?

The Second Law requires that some heat must be rejected for any cycle to operate. Real processes are irreversible and generate entropy. Power plant designers must increase the temperature difference between heat source and sink to improve efficiency, and operators focus on minimizing losses through maintenance and proper operation.

---

## Flashcard ID: 7
### Question: What are the key differences between the Rankine cycle and the Carnot cycle?

Rankine is more practical: it uses constant-pressure (isobaric) heat transfer at phase boundaries instead of Carnot's constant-temperature processes. Rankine can be easily analyzed using steam tables. Rankine efficiency is lower than Carnot but achievable in actual power plants.

---

## Flashcard ID: 9
### Question: Define condensate depression with a numerical example.

Condensate depression = Saturation temperature at condenser pressure - Hotwell temperature. Example: At 1 psia absolute (saturation temp ≈ 102°F), if hotwell temperature is 96°F, then condensate depression = 102°F - 96°F = 6°F (six degrees of depression).

---

## Flashcard ID: 10
### Question: List five operational methods to maintain unit efficiency.

1) Minimize auxiliary equipment operation to only those necessary for current power output, 2) Minimize steam generator blowdown, 3) Fix steam leaks immediately, 4) Eliminate air leaks into the condenser, 5) Operate air ejector, gland seal, and blowdown heat exchangers to maximize heat recovery.

---

## Flashcard ID: 11
### Question: How do operators monitor and assess plant efficiency?

Operators compare electrical generator output (proportional to turbine work) to reactor power output (proportional to heat supplied). Lower-than-expected electrical output for a given reactor power indicates secondary system inefficiency requiring investigation and maintenance.
