# CP01_Piping_Valves_and_Actuators - Quick Reference Flashcards

---
### Question
What is the purpose of piping in a power plant?

### Answer
To provide housing through which fluids (and sometimes fluid/solid mixtures) are transported from one place to another.

---
id: 234
---
### Question
Name the design factors used to size and select piping.

### Answer
Fluid temperature, fluid pressure, fluid volume/mass flow rate, chemical properties, physical properties, fluid contamination, shock loads, and changes in direction.

---
id: 235
---
### Question
Why is low-carbon steel used for water and low-temperature steam lines?

### Answer
It's low cost and easy to work with, though it rusts easily.

---
id: 236
---
### Question
Which piping material is used where corrosion product production near the reactor is a concern?

### Answer
Stainless steel.

---
id: 237
---
### Question
What are snubbers used for in piping systems?

### Answer
They limit seismic motion while allowing thermal growth.

---
id: 238
---
### Question
Name four things you should never do to a piping support/restraint (without a procedure/work request).

### Answer
Climb on them, attach rigging or valve chains to them, adjust them, or remove them.

---
id: 239
---
### Question
What are thermal loops and expansion joints used for?

### Answer
To compensate for forces resulting from thermal expansion/contraction of piping.

---
id: 240
---
### Question
What happens to differential pressure (D/P) as a valve closes?

### Answer
D/P, backpressure, and headloss across the valve increase, while flow and downstream pressure decrease.

---
id: 241
---
### Question
Name the five basic parts of a valve.

### Answer
Body, bonnet, disk, seat rings, and stem.

---
id: 242
---
### Question
What is throttling?

### Answer
The flow restriction caused by partially closing a valve, which increases D/P and reduces downstream pressure and flow.

---
id: 243
---
### Question
What is the primary purpose of a valve's backseat?

### Answer
To remove system pressure from the packing when packing leakage is excessive and adjustment can't correct it.

---
id: 244
---
### Question
What can happen if you backseat a motor operated valve (MOV)?

### Answer
It can result in the valve being unable to close on demand.

---
id: 245
---
### Question
What is the difference between a rising stem and non-rising stem valve?

### Answer
In a rising stem valve, the stem itself moves (rises) as it's turned. In a non-rising stem valve, a threaded sleeve connected to the disk rises, but the stem does not.

---
id: 246
---
### Question
What is the stuffing box?

### Answer
The area in the bonnet that contains the packing material surrounding the valve stem.

---
id: 247
---
### Question
Why are gate valves poor throttle valves?

### Answer
Positioning the disk partway in the flow stream causes turbulence that accelerates wear and can damage the valve.

---
id: 248
---
### Question
What type of valve is used almost exclusively as an isolation valve?

### Answer
The gate valve.

---
id: 249
---
### Question
Over what portion of gate valve opening does most of the system flow change occur?

### Answer
The first 25% of opening produces significantly more flow change than the last 25% (75-100% open).

---
id: 250
---
### Question
Why do globe valves have a high pressure loss even when fully open?

### Answer
The seat is perpendicular to (or at a sharp angle to) flow, creating a tortuous flow path.

---
id: 251
---
### Question
What is the preferred flow direction through a globe valve, and why?

### Answer
Up through the seat -- it reduces disk vibration and removes pressure from the stem packing when closed.

---
id: 252
---
### Question
What makes a Kerotest valve different from a standard globe valve?

### Answer
It's a 'pack-less' globe valve that uses diaphragms as the pressure boundary instead of relying on stem packing alone.

---
id: 253
---
### Question
How many turns does a properly adjusted Kerotest valve need to go from full open to full closed?

### Answer
About 1 1/4 turns.

---
id: 254
---
### Question
Why should a Kerotest valve never be used for reverse flow?

### Answer
The small disk-to-seat clearance can cause severe vibration/chattering that may fail nearby welds.

---
id: 255
---
### Question
What was the root cause of NCR 01656020 (loss of seal injection)?

### Answer
Internal binding in Kerotest valve 2NV-249 at approximately half-open on the handwheel travel, which the operators mistook for fully open.

---
id: 256
---
### Question
What are needle valves used for?

### Answer
Making fine, precise adjustments to flow, such as in sampling lines -- they are poor isolation valves.

---
id: 257
---
### Question
What kind of seat do butterfly valves typically use?

### Answer
A soft, resilient seat made of rubber or neoprene.

---
id: 258
---
### Question
In what type of systems are butterfly valves commonly used?

### Answer
High-flow, large-diameter, low-pressure systems such as circulating water.

---
id: 259
---
### Question
What is the main functional limitation of plug and ball valves?

### Answer
They can only stop or start flow -- they cannot regulate/throttle flow.

---
id: 260
---
### Question
How can you tell if a ball valve is open or closed by its handle?

### Answer
The handle lines up with the pipe when open, and is 90 degrees to the pipe when closed.

---
id: 261
---
### Question
Why are diaphragm valves used for radioactive or corrosive fluids?

### Answer
The fluid is completely separated from the moving valve parts, so they aren't subject to contamination or degeneration.

---
id: 262
---
### Question
Name the three basic types of check valves.

### Answer
Swing check, lift check, and stop check.

---
id: 263
---
### Question
What makes a stop check valve different from a lift check valve?

### Answer
The stem extends out to an actuator, so it can be forcibly closed and stop flow in both directions.

---
id: 264
---
### Question
What is 'accumulation' in relief valve terminology?

### Answer
The pressure above the preset setpoint at which the relief valve is fully open, expressed as a percent of preset pressure.

---
id: 265
---
### Question
What is 'blowdown' in safety valve terminology?

### Answer
The difference between the preset setpoint and the actual reseating pressure, expressed as a percent of preset pressure.

---
id: 266
---
### Question
What is the key functional difference between a safety valve and a relief valve?

### Answer
A safety valve opens quickly (pops open) with little/no accumulation; a relief valve opens gradually with accumulation as pressure rises.

---
id: 267
---
### Question
Why are relief valves mainly used on liquid systems and safety valves mainly on steam/gas systems?

### Answer
Mass flow/loss is lower for steam than water, and pressure drops faster when a water-filled vessel's valve lifts than one passing steam.

---
id: 268
---
### Question
What does a PORV on the pressurizer relieve, and why?

### Answer
Steam, not water -- this allows rapid energy release with less coolant inventory loss for primary system overpressure protection.

---
id: 269
---
### Question
What is a gagging device used for?

### Answer
To lock a relief/safety valve stem down (via a bolt through the bonnet) so it cannot lift, typically during hydrostatic testing above the normal lift setting.

---
id: 270
---
### Question
A spring applies 1200 lbf to a 3-inch diameter disk (7.07 in^2). What is the opening pressure?

### Answer
170 psi (1200 lbf / 7.07 in^2).

### Question
A safety valve's setpoint is 1200 psia and it's fully open at 1242 psia. What is the percent accumulation?

### Answer
3.5% -- (1242-1200)/1200 x 100%.

### Question
A safety valve opens at 1200 psi and closes at 1140 psi. What is the percent blowdown?

### Answer
5% -- (1200-1140)/1200 x 100%.

---
id: 273
---
### Question
What does a vacuum breaker protect against?

### Answer
It protects tanks/vessels from collapsing due to low-pressure (vacuum) conditions, by admitting ambient pressure.

---
id: 274
---
### Question
What is the purpose of a steam trap?

### Answer
To remove condensate from a steam line while preventing loss of steam -- important to keep water out of the main turbine.

---
id: 275
---
### Question
How does a bellows-type steam trap work?

### Answer
Steam heats a volatile liquid in a sealed bellows, which expands and closes the valve; as steam condenses and the bellows cools/contracts, the valve opens to drain condensate.

### Question
What are the four basic types of valve actuators?

### Answer
Mechanical (manual), electrical, pneumatic, and hydraulic.

---
id: 277
---
### Question
What does 'fail as is' mean, and what type of valve is it most associated with?

### Answer
The valve remains in its current position if motive force is lost -- most often associated with motor operated valves (MOVs).

---
id: 278
---
### Question
What is the difference between 'fail open/closed' and 'fail safe'?

### Answer
Fail open/closed describe the actual position on loss of power; fail safe is the design requirement for which position (open or closed) protects the system, which then determines whether the valve is built to fail open or fail closed.

---
id: 279
---
### Question
Why should the MOV declutch lever never be operated while the motor is running?

### Answer
It could damage the clutch mechanism.

---
id: 280
---
### Question
What safety practice resulted from the Crystal River event?

### Answer
The use of valve wrenches to manually operate motor operated valves (MOVs) is prohibited.

---
id: 281
---
### Question
What determines a solenoid valve's failure position?

### Answer
Spring placement -- solenoid valves are typically fully open or fully closed.

---
id: 282
---
### Question
What are the four basic types of pneumatic actuators?

### Answer
Gas cylinder, gas piston, gas diaphragm, and gas vane.

---
id: 283
---
### Question
How do you determine how a pneumatic valve will fail on loss of power?

### Answer
Locate the gas solenoid valve in the supply line (labeled FO or FC) and determine which direction the spring will drive the valve if gas is lost.

---
id: 284
---
### Question
What indicates valve position by remote/local light?

### Answer
Limit switches attached to the valve stem -- red light for open, green light for closed, both lit typically means mid-position.

---
id: 285
---
### Question
When locally verifying a valve's position, which direction should you always attempt to move it first, and why?

### Answer
The closed direction -- so you don't accidentally initiate unwanted flow by opening a valve that was actually closed.

---
id: 286
---
### Question
How do you check if a butterfly or plug valve is open using its operating lever?

### Answer
If the lever is parallel to the piping, the valve is open; if perpendicular, it's closed.

---
id: 287
---
### Question
What is thermal binding?

### Answer
When a valve body cools faster than its disk, causing the seat to bind against the disk -- closing hot and cooling can make the valve impossible to reopen until the body is reheated.

---
id: 288
---
### Question
What is pressure (bonnet) locking?

### Answer
Liquid trapped in the valve bonnet pressurizes as it heats; without a relief path, the pressure buildup can prevent valve operation.

---
id: 289
---
### Question
How can thermal binding be prevented during a cooldown?

### Answer
By periodically cracking the affected valve open and re-shutting it, and by using proper closing torque.

---
id: 290
---
### Question
Why should you never use cheater bars or multiple operators to free a stuck valve?

### Answer
They can cause excessive torque on the valve stem, potentially damaging it.

---
id: 291
---
### Question
What direction do you typically turn a manual handwheel to open a valve?

### Answer
Counterclockwise (clockwise to shut).
