# TH04_Thermodynamics_III - Exam Questions

## Question ID: 49
### Question: In a process where no heat is transferred to or from the system, what term describes this condition?

**Options:**
- a) Isothermal
- b) Isobaric
- c) Adiabatic
- d) Isochoric


**Correct:** c
**Explanation:** Adiabatic process: Q = 0 (no heat transfer). Temperature and pressure can change in adiabatic processes.

## Question ID: 50
### Question: For a turbine, the work output is typically expressed as the difference between which two values?

**Options:**
- a) Hout - Hin
- b) Hin - Hout
- c) Pin - Pout
- d) Tin - Tout


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Turbine work: W = h_in - h_out. The enthalpy drop across the turbine equals the mechanical work output.

---

## Question ID: 51
### Question: What does the term 'isenthalpic' describe in a thermodynamic process?

**Options:**
- a) No change in entropy
- b) Constant enthalpy (h_in = h_out)
- c) Constant pressure
- d) Constant temperature


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Isenthalpic process: enthalpy remains constant. Throttling (through a valve) is an isenthalpic process.

---

## Question ID: 52
### Question: Which of the following is the correct expression for the work input to a pump?

**Options:**
- a) Hin - Hout
- b) Hout - Hin
- c) Pin - Pout
- d) Tin - Tout


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Pump work: W = h_out - h_in (positive value). Work is input to increase fluid pressure and enthalpy.

## Question ID: 53
### Question: Condensate depression is defined as the difference between which two temperatures?

**Options:**
- a) Boiling point - Room temperature
- b) Tsat - Tcondensate
- c) Inlet temperature - Outlet temperature
- d) Condenser temperature - Cooling water temperature


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Condensate depression = saturation temperature minus actual condensate temperature. A few degrees prevents pump cavitation.

## Question ID: 54
### Question: A process that occurs at constant pressure is called:

**Options:**
- a) Isothermal
- b) Isochoric
- c) Isobaric
- d) Adiabatic


**Correct:** c
**Explanation:** Isobaric process: P = constant. Boiler heat addition and condenser heat rejection are isobaric processes.

## Question ID: 55

**Options:**
- a) Isobaric
- b) Isochoric

- d) Isentropic


**Correct:** c
**Explanation:** Isothermal process: T = constant. Often occurs during condensation or with isothermal compression/expansion.

## Question ID: 56
### Question: What does the Second Law of Thermodynamics state about a heat engine operating in a cycle?

**Options:**
- a) All heat can be converted to work
- b) No engine, actual or ideal, can convert all the heat supplied into work; some heat must always be rejected
- c) Heat only flows from cold to hot
- d) The amount of entropy always decreases


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Second Law: No cyclic heat engine can achieve 100% efficiency. All engines must reject heat to the surroundings.

## Question ID: 57
### Question: The change in entropy of a system is defined as which of the following?

**Options:**
- a) The heat added to the system multiplied by temperature
- b) The heat transferred to or from the system divided by the absolute temperature at which the transfer occurs
- c) The change in internal energy of the system
- d) The rate of heat transfer


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Entropy change: ΔS = Q/T_abs. Total entropy increases in any real irreversible process (Second Law).

## Question ID: 58
### Question: In a convergent nozzle, what happens to the velocity and pressure of the fluid?

**Options:**
- a) Both velocity and pressure increase
- b) Velocity increases and pressure decreases
- c) Velocity decreases and pressure increases
- d) Both velocity and pressure decrease


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** In convergent nozzles: flow area decreases → velocity increases, pressure decreases (kinetic energy increases).

## Question ID: 59
### Question: Why is a few degrees of condensate subcooling desirable at the condenser hotwell?

**Options:**
- a) To increase system temperature
- b) To prevent cavitation in the condensate pumps
- c) To reduce overall system pressure
- d) To improve condenser heat transfer


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Condensate depression prevents pump cavitation (vapor bubbles) but excessive depression wastes heat and increases corrosion.

## Question ID: 60
### Question: An ideal turbine or pump work process is described as isentropic because:

**Options:**
- a) Temperature remains constant
- b) Friction is not considered, so there is no change in entropy of the working fluid
- c) Pressure remains constant
- d) No work is performed


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Isentropic process: ΔS = 0 (reversible). Real turbines/pumps add entropy through friction, reducing their efficiency.

---

## Question ID: 61
### Question: Turbine efficiency is defined as which of the following ratios?

**Options:**
- a) Ideal work / Real work
- b) Real work / Ideal work
- c) Output power / Input power
- d) Pressure drop / Enthalpy drop


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Turbine efficiency = Real work output / Ideal isentropic work. Real turbines are always less efficient than ideal.

## Question ID: 100
### Question: Why is the First Law of Thermodynamics essential for analyzing heat exchangers?

**Options:**
- a) It predicts the type of heat exchanger needed
- b) It relates energy input and output; for a heat exchanger, heat gained by one fluid equals heat lost by the other (no work done)
- c) It determines the pressure drop
- d) It measures vibration levels


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** In heat exchangers: Q gained = Q lost (First Law). The energy equation q = h_out - h_in applies directly.

## Question ID: 101
### Question: What is the function of a divergent nozzle?

**Options:**
- a) To increase velocity and decrease pressure
- b) To decrease velocity and increase pressure
- c) To maintain constant velocity
- d) To isolate fluid flows


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** In a divergent nozzle: flow area increases → velocity decreases, pressure increases (kinetic energy converts to pressure energy).

## Question ID: 102
### Question: What process occurs when steam flows through a partially open valve, resulting in pressure drop and entropy increase?

**Options:**
- a) Isentropic expansion
- b) Throttling (isenthalpic process)
- c) Isothermal expansion
- d) Adiabatic expansion


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Throttling: constant enthalpy (h_in = h_out), pressure drops, entropy increases. No work done, no heat added. Used for flow control.

---

## Question ID: 103
### Question: What is pump efficiency defined as?

**Options:**
- a) Real work output / Ideal work
- b) Ideal work (fluid power) / Real work (shaft power)
- c) Inlet pressure / Outlet pressure
- d) Flow rate / Pressure rise


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Pump efficiency = ideal (isentropic) work / real (shaft) work. Real pumps require more shaft power than ideal due to friction.

---

## Question ID: 104
### Question: How does superheating steam in the boiler affect turbine work output?

**Options:**
- a) It decreases turbine work
- b) It increases turbine work and also improves cycle efficiency
- c) It has no effect on turbine work
- d) It only affects condenser pressure


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Superheating increases the enthalpy of steam entering the turbine (h_in increases), resulting in greater work output (W = h_in - h_out).

---

## Question ID: 105
### Question: What is the effect of friction losses in a real turbine compared to an ideal isentropic turbine?

**Options:**
- a) Real turbine produces more work
- b) Real turbine produces less work and adds entropy to the system
- c) Friction has no effect
- d) Real turbine has lower outlet pressure


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Friction in real turbines increases entropy (ΔS > 0), reducing the enthalpy drop available for work. Turbine efficiency accounts for this.
