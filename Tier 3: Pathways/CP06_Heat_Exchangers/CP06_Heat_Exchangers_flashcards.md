### Question
What is the main difference between direct and indirect contact heat exchangers?

### Answer
Direct: fluids touch each other. Indirect: fluids separated by solid surface (tube/plate).

---
id: 187
---
### Question
What are common examples of direct contact heat exchangers?

### Answer
Cooling towers, lakes, rivers. Fluids in direct contact.

---
id: 188
---
### Question
What are common examples of indirect contact heat exchangers?

### Answer
Steam generators, main condensers. Fluids separated by surfaces.

### Question
How does a forced draft cooling tower work?

### Answer
Fans blow air into tower to exchange heat between water and air.

---
id: 190
---
### Question
How does a natural draft cooling tower work?

### Answer
Convective chimney effect (hot air rises) pulls air through. No fans needed.

---
id: 191
---
### Question
What two flow configurations are used in cooling towers?

### Answer
Cross-flow (air enters side, exits opposite) and Counter-flow (air enters bottom, exits top).

---
id: 192
---
### Question
What is the purpose of cooling tower fill material?

### Answer
Increases air-water contact surface area. Better heat transfer. Usually PVC/plastic.

### Question
What are the four basic flow paths in shell-and-tube heat exchangers?

### Answer
Cross flow, Parallel flow, Counter flow, U-tube.

### Question
What is crossflow heat transfer?

### Answer
Fluids flow perpendicular to each other. Example: main condensers.

---
id: 195
---
### Question
What is parallel flow heat transfer?

### Answer
Fluids flow in same direction. Requires large surface area. Rarely used.

---
id: 196
---
### Question
What is counter flow heat transfer?

### Answer
Fluids flow in opposite directions. Most efficient. Maintains large temp difference.

---
id: 197
---
### Question
Why is counter-flow more efficient than parallel-flow?

### Answer
Maintains larger temp difference throughout, allows more heat transfer in smaller space.

---
id: 198
---
### Question
What is the formula for heat transfer rate?

### Answer
Q = Uo × A × ΔT. (coefficient × area × temp difference).

---
id: 199
---
### Question
What are three factors that increase heat transfer rate?

### Answer
Larger Uo (coefficient), larger A (area), larger ΔT (temperature difference).

---
id: 200
---
### Question
What is a regenerative heat exchanger?

### Answer
Transfers heat from hot to cold sections of SAME system. Example: Feedwater Heaters. Minimizes energy loss.

---
id: 201
---
### Question
What is a non-regenerative heat exchanger?

### Answer
Transfers heat between TWO SEPARATE systems. Heat rejected/discarded. Example: Lube Oil Coolers.

---

---
### Question
What are the major components of shell-and-tube heat exchangers?

### Answer
Shell, tube bundle, tubesheets, waterboxes, baffles, nozzles, vents, drains.

---
id: 203
---
### Question
What is the shell in a heat exchanger?

### Answer
Casing surrounding heat transfer region. Houses shell-side fluid.

---
id: 204
---
### Question
What are tubesheets?

### Answer
Perforated metal plates at shell ends. Separate fluids and support tubes.

---
id: 205
---
### Question
What are baffles?

### Answer
Internal dividers directing shell-side flow to cross tubes multiple times.

---
id: 206
---
### Question
What are two common tube types?

### Answer
Straight tubes (easy clean/replace) and U-tubes (simplified expansion, fewer joints).

---
id: 207
---
### Question
What is thermal shock in a heat exchanger?

### Answer
Sudden temp change causing excessive thermal stress. Risk of cracking/brittle fracture.

---
id: 208
---
### Question
When is thermal shock most likely?

### Answer
Startup: hot/cold fluids suddenly introduced. Sudden temp changes during operation.

---
id: 209
---
### Question
How do you prevent thermal shock during startup?

### Answer
Gradually introduce fluids by throttling inlet valves. Minimize sudden temp changes.

---
id: 210
---
### Question
What is the startup procedure for a heat exchanger?

### Answer
(1) Circulate cold fluid with vents open. (2) Slowly valve in hot fluid. (3) Throttle inlet to prevent pressure spikes.

---
id: 211
---
### Question
What is water hammer in heat exchangers?

### Answer
Pressure spike when fluid velocity suddenly changes. Causes pipe movement/damage.

---
id: 212
---
### Question
How do you minimize water hammer?

### Answer
Operate valves slowly. Avoid sudden velocity changes.

---
id: 213
---
### Question
How do flow rates affect heat exchanger performance?

### Answer
Too high: excessive pressure drops, energy loss. Too low: inadequate heat transfer. Must optimize.

---
id: 214
---
### Question
What is scaling in a heat exchanger?

### Answer
Deposit buildup (mineral scale, corrosion). Reduces heat transfer surface/efficiency.

---
id: 215
---
### Question
What causes tube fouling?

### Answer
Mineral deposits, corrosion products, biological growth. Reduces coefficient/area.

---
id: 216
---
### Question
What happens if a heat exchanger tube fails?

### Answer
Cross-contamination of fluids. Loss of heat transfer. Operational hazards.

---
id: 217
---
### Question
How do you maintain heat exchanger performance?

### Answer
Keep surfaces clean. Maintain proper flow. Monitor fouling. Detect failures early.

---
id: 218
---
### Question
What is the relationship between condenser vacuum and turbine backpressure?

### Answer
Higher vacuum (lower pressure) = lower backpressure = greater pressure difference = more turbine work/efficiency.

---
id: 219
---
### Question
Why is condenser vacuum important?

### Answer
Better vacuum improves turbine efficiency. Increases pressure difference. More steam work.

---
id: 220
---
### Question
What happens when condenser vacuum degrades?

### Answer
Backpressure increases. Turbine output decreases. Plant efficiency reduces.

---
id: 221
---
### Question
How is vacuum measured in condensers?

### Answer
As absolute pressure. Lower absolute pressure = better vacuum.

---
id: 222
---
### Question
How does a main condenser operate?

### Answer
Exhaust steam contacts cold tubes → condenses to condensate. Cooling water absorbs latent heat.

---
id: 223
---
### Question
What is the flow pattern in a main condenser?

### Answer
Crossflow: steam flows down perpendicular to cooling water tubes.

---
id: 224
---
### Question
What is the hotwell?

### Answer
Collects condensed steam at condenser bottom. Pumped to feedwater system.

---
id: 225
---
### Question
How much cooling water is needed per pound of steam?

### Answer
65-100 lbm (8-12 gallons) cooling water per lbm steam condensed.

---
id: 226
---
### Question
What are non-condensable gases?

### Answer
Air and other gases that cannot condense. Accumulate and increase pressure. Reduce vacuum.

---
id: 227
---
### Question
How do air removal systems work?

### Answer
Jet air ejectors or vacuum pumps remove non-condensable gases continuously.

---
id: 228
---
### Question
How do you monitor condenser efficiency?

### Answer
Measure vacuum, outlet water temp, flow rates. Degradation: reduced vacuum, higher outlet temp.

---
id: 229
---
### Question
What is biofouling?

### Answer
Aquatic organism growth. Degrades cooling tower/condenser. Controlled by chemical treatment.

---

---
### Question
What are three key principles of heat exchanger operation?

### Answer
(1) Heat transfers hot → cold. (2) Temp difference drives transfer. (3) Minimize losses through design/maintenance.

---
id: 231
---
### Question
What makes an effective heat exchanger?

### Answer
Proper flow patterns, adequate surface area, clean surfaces, correct fluid properties, good thermal design.

### Question
What is an operator's role in heat exchanger performance?

### Answer
Monitor conditions, maintain flow, prevent fouling, detect failures, proper startup/shutdown.
