### Question
What defines the state of a substance?

### Answer
Two independent, intensive properties (e.g. specific enthalpy and specific entropy).

---
id: 293
---
### Question
What's the difference between state and phase?

### Answer
State is defined by properties (pressure, temperature, etc.); phase describes molecular structure (solid, liquid, gas).

---
id: 294
---
### Question
What is sublimation?

### Answer
A direct change from solid phase to gas phase, with no liquid phase present.

---
id: 295
---
### Question
What is Avogadro's Number?

### Answer
6.023 x 10^23 -- the number of carbon atoms in 12 grams of carbon-12; one mole of any substance has this many atoms/molecules.

---
id: 296
---
### Question
State Charles' Law.

### Answer
At low pressures, the volume of a gas at constant pressure is directly proportional to its absolute temperature.

---
id: 297
---
### Question
State Boyle's Law.

### Answer
At low pressures, the volume of a gas at constant temperature is inversely proportional to its absolute pressure.

---
id: 298
---
### Question
What is the Ideal Gas Law equation?

### Answer
PV = nRT, where R is the ideal gas constant.

---
id: 299
---
### Question
Why shouldn't the Ideal Gas Law be used for steam properties?

### Answer
The equation of state for a vapor is much more complicated than for an ideal gas; steam tables (based on extensive experimental measurement) should be used instead.

---
id: 300
---
### Question
What are the three phase lines on a P-T diagram?

### Answer
Sublimation line (solid-gas), fusion line (liquid-solid), and vaporization line (liquid-gas).

---
id: 301
---
### Question
What's unusual about water's fusion line compared to most substances?

### Answer
It curves LEFT, so water's melting point DECREASES at higher pressure (most substances' fusion lines curve right).

---
id: 302
---
### Question
What is the Critical Point?

### Answer
The highest temperature and pressure at which liquid and gas can exist in equilibrium as distinguishable phases; above it, latent heat of vaporization is zero.

---
id: 303
---
### Question
What are water's critical temperature and pressure?

### Answer
705.47F and 3,208.2 psia.

---
id: 304
---
### Question
What is the Triple Point?

### Answer
The single point where solid, liquid, and gas can all exist together in equilibrium; for water, 32.02F and 0.089 psia.

---
id: 305
---
### Question
What is the vapor dome on a P-v diagram?

### Answer
The region bounded by the saturated liquid and saturated vapor lines, where liquid and vapor coexist in equilibrium.

---
id: 306
---
### Question
What's the difference between steam quality and moisture content?

### Answer
Quality (X) is the fraction of vapor present; moisture content (M) is the fraction of liquid present. X = 1 - M.

---
id: 307
---
### Question
What does 100% steam quality mean?

### Answer
Dry steam -- 0% moisture content, no liquid present.

---
id: 308
---
### Question
Why do power plant designers care about steam quality entering a turbine?

### Answer
Blading damage occurs on most high-pressure turbines when steam quality drops below about 99%.

---
id: 309
---
### Question
Within the vapor dome, are pressure and temperature independent properties?

### Answer
No -- fixing one fixes the other; quality or moisture content is needed as an additional property to determine the actual state.

---
id: 310
---
### Question
What is a subcooled (compressed) liquid?

### Answer
A liquid below its saturation temperature for its pressure, or above its saturation pressure for its temperature.

---
id: 311
---
### Question
What is a saturated liquid?

### Answer
A liquid at its boiling point -- any further heat addition causes evaporation rather than a temperature rise.

---
id: 312
---
### Question
What is latent heat?

### Answer
Heat that increases a fluid's enthalpy without changing its temperature, associated with a phase change.

---
id: 313
---
### Question
What is the latent heat of vaporization?

### Answer
The heat required to convert saturated liquid completely to saturated vapor at constant temperature and pressure.

---
id: 314
---
### Question
What is a superheated vapor?

### Answer
Steam above its boiling (saturation) temperature for its pressure.

---
id: 315
---
### Question
How do you calculate degrees of superheat?

### Answer
Actual temperature minus the saturation temperature (Tsat) for that pressure.

---
id: 316
---
### Question
Water at atmospheric pressure is at 112F. How many degrees is it subcooled?

### Answer
100F (212F boiling point - 112F actual = 100F of subcooling).

---
id: 317
---
### Question
What diagram should be used to analyze thermodynamic cycles, and why?

### Answer
The T-s (Temperature-Entropy) diagram -- the area under the curve represents heat transferred into or out of the system.

---
id: 318
---
### Question
What are the two main sets of tables in the Steam Tables?

### Answer
Saturated steam tables (temperature-indexed and pressure-indexed) and superheated steam tables.

---
id: 319
---
### Question
How is the specific volume of a wet steam mixture calculated?

### Answer
v = vL + X(vV - vL), where X is the quality and vL/vV are the saturated liquid/vapor specific volumes.

---
id: 320
---
### Question
What is supersaturation?

### Answer
When a vapor undergoes an extremely rapid pressure drop (e.g. across a turbine nozzle) faster than condensation can occur, so it briefly behaves like superheated steam even inside the wet vapor region.

---
id: 321
---
### Question
What's the difference between the Saturated Steam Tables and Compressed Liquid Tables?

### Answer
Saturated tables give properties at the boiling point for a given temperature/pressure; Compressed Liquid Tables give exact subcooled-liquid properties away from saturation.

---
id: 322
---
### Question
What can happen if a pump doesn't get adequately subcooled liquid at its suction?

### Answer
Cavitation -- liquid flashes to vapor at the low-pressure suction, eroding/pitting internal components, and in severe cases can vapor-lock the pump.

---
id: 323
---
### Question
What kind of plot is a Mollier diagram?

### Answer
An Enthalpy-Entropy (h-s) plot for steam.

---
id: 324
---
### Question
What's the main advantage of a Mollier diagram?

### Answer
Quickly finding steam properties from two of three known values (quality, pressure, temperature) without consulting multiple steam tables.

---
id: 325
---
### Question
What's the main disadvantage of a Mollier diagram?

### Answer
It's difficult to read values accurately, especially by interpolating between isotherms/isobars -- steam tables should be used when accuracy matters.

---
id: 326
---
### Question
What caused reactor coolant pump RC-3A to cavitate at Fort Calhoun in 1998?

### Answer
An incorrectly revised procedure directed operators to use pressurizer steam-space (instead of water-space) temperature to determine pressure, making them believe pressure was over 100 psi higher than actual.

---
id: 327
---
### Question
What was the root cause of the Fort Calhoun RCP cavitation event?

### Answer
Inadequate technical review of a procedure revision that changed 'water' to 'steam' space temperature without being caught.
