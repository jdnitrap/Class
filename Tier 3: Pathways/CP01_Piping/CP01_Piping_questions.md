# CP01_Piping - Exam Questions

## Question ID: 1
### Question: What is the primary purpose of piping in a power plant?

**Options:**
- a) To transport fluids from one piece of equipment to another
- b) To regulate system pressure
- c) To store fluids for later use
- d) To measure fluid temperature

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** a

**Explanation:** Piping serves as the housing to transport fluids (and sometimes fluid/solid mixtures) between equipment in power plant systems.

## Question ID: 4
### Question: Which of the following is NOT one of the five principal functions of valves?

**Options:**
- a) Starting/stopping flow
- b) Throttling flow
- c) Increasing system pressure
- d) Relieving pressure

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** c

**Explanation:** The five principal valve functions are: starting/stopping, throttling, controlling flow direction, regulating pressure, and relieving pressure. Increasing system pressure is not a valve function.

## Question ID: 6
### Question: Gate valves are primarily used as isolation valves rather than for throttling because:

**Options:**
- a) They are more expensive than globe valves
- b) Positioning the disk in the flow stream causes turbulence and accelerated wear
- c) They cannot withstand high pressures
- d) They are harder to operate

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** b

**Explanation:** Gate valves are unsuitable for throttling because disk positioning in the flow causes turbulence and accelerated wear, damaging the valve.

## Question ID: 14
### Question: What is the key difference between a safety valve and a relief valve?

**Options:**
- a) Safety valves are used for liquids, relief valves for gases
- b) Safety valves pop fully open with little or no accumulation, while relief valves require accumulation
- c) Relief valves pop fully open, safety valves open gradually

## Question ID: 74
### Question: What is thermal binding in valves?

**Options:**
- a) The bonnet expanding and blocking the stem
- b) Differential thermal contraction between the valve body and disk, potentially binding the disk to the seat
- c) Heat causing the disk to warp
- d) Stem corrosion during shutdown

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** b

**Explanation:** Thermal binding occurs when the valve body cools faster than the disk, causing the disk to bind against the seat. Prevention: crack open periodically during cooldown.

**Options:**
- a) External pressure preventing the valve from opening
- b) Liquid trapped in the valve bonnet pressurizing as it heats, preventing the valve from opening
- c) The disk corroding to the seat
- d) Excessive handwheel torque

**Correct:** a
**Correct:** b

**Explanation:** Pressure locking occurs when trapped liquid in the bonnet heats and pressurizes, making the valve impossible to open. Prevention: provide bonnet relief or vent paths.
