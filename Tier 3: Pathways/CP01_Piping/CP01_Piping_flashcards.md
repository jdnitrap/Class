### Question
What is the primary purpose of piping systems?

### Answer
Transport fluids/fluid-solid mixtures between equipment. Key factors: temperature, pressure, flow rate, shock loads.

---
id: 2
---
### Question
What five design factors affect pipe material selection?

### Answer
Temperature, pressure, flow rate/velocity, shock loads (water/steam hammer, thermal shock), directional changes.

---
id: 3
---
### Question
Which piping material is economical but prone to rust and corrosion?

### Answer
Low-carbon steel.

---
id: 4
---
### Question
What material is best for high-temperature and high-pressure applications?

### Answer
Nickel alloys (high-cost) or low-alloy steel.

---
id: 5
---
### Question
What are the three types of pipe support systems?

### Answer
Rigid restraints (fixed), spring hangers (allow thermal movement), snubbers (limit seismic motion).

---
id: 6
---
### Question
How do piping systems accommodate thermal expansion?

### Answer
Thermal loops/bends or expansion joints allow controlled linear expansion.

---

---
### Question
What are the five principal valve functions?

### Answer
Start/stop flow, throttle flow, control direction, regulate pressure, relieve pressure.

---
id: 8
---
### Question
What type of valve is used to start or stop complete fluid flow?

### Answer
Gate valves, ball valves, or plug valves.

---
id: 9
---
### Question
What type of valve controls flow rate without completely stopping flow?

### Answer
Globe valves or needle valves.

---
id: 10
---
### Question
Name three basic valve construction components.

### Answer
Body (flow path/seats), bonnet (sealing section), disk (opens/closes/throttles).

---
id: 11
---
### Question
What is the purpose of the backseat in a valve?

### Answer
Allows packing adjustment without depressurizing system.

---
id: 12
---
### Question
What is the difference between rising-stem and non-rising stem valves?

### Answer
Rising-stem: moves upward (visual indicator). Non-rising: position not visually obvious.

### Question
What is pressure defined as?

### Answer
Pressure = Force / Area (P = F/A). Units: psi, Pa, bar, atm.

---
id: 14
---
### Question
What is normal atmospheric pressure in psia and kPa?

### Answer
14.7 psia = 101.3 kPa.

---
id: 15
---
### Question
What is hydrostatic pressure?

### Answer
Pressure from fluid column weight above reference point (P = ρ × g × h).

---
id: 16
---
### Question
What is absolute pressure used for?

### Answer
Thermodynamic calculations; measured from perfect vacuum. Example: 10 psig = 24.7 psia.

---
id: 17
---
### Question
What is gage pressure (PSIG)?

### Answer
Pressure above atmospheric (Pgage = Pabs - Patm). Common gauge reading.

---
id: 18
---
### Question
What is differential pressure (PSID)?

### Answer
Difference between two locations. Used for: filter drop, valve throttling, level measurement.

---
id: 19
---
### Question
What does Pascal's Law state?

### Answer
Pressure in confined fluid transmits undiminished in all directions (F1/A1 = F2/A2).

---
id: 20
---
### Question
What is thermal binding in valves?

### Answer
Body cools faster than disk → differential contraction binds seat to disk → valve may not reopen.

---
id: 21
---
### Question
How can thermal binding be prevented?

### Answer
Crack valve open/re-shut during cooldown. Use proper closing torque. Allow gradual cooling.

---
id: 22
---
### Question
What is pressure locking in valves?

### Answer
Liquid trapped in bonnet pressurizes when heated → prevents valve opening (high-temp systems).

---
id: 23
---
### Question
How is pressure locking prevented?

### Answer
Provide relief or vent path on bonnet to equalize pressure buildup.

---
id: 24
---
### Question
Should a wrench ever be used on a valve handwheel for extra leverage?

### Answer
No. Can overstress actuator gearing/casing → catastrophic failure.

---
id: 25
---
### Question
What does reaching hard stop on a handwheel confirm?

### Answer
Nothing. Must verify full travel by counting designed turns; position limit switch is unreliable.
