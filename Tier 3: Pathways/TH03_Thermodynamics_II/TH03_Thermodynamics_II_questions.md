# TH03_Thermodynamics_II - Exam Questions

## Question ID: 40
### Question: What is the term for a liquid existing at a temperature below its saturation temperature for a given pressure?

**Options:**
- a) Saturated liquid
- b) Subcooled (or compressed) liquid
- c) Superheated liquid
- d) Critical liquid


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Subcooled (or compressed) liquid is below saturation temperature for its pressure, meaning it could absorb heat and vaporize.

## Question ID: 41
### Question: What is the term for steam that is 100% vapor at its boiling temperature, with no liquid remaining?

**Options:**
- a) Saturated vapor
- b) Superheated vapor
- c) Wet vapor
- d) Critical vapor

**Correct:** a

**Explanation:** Saturated vapor (dry steam) is at saturation temperature with quality X = 100%. Any further heating produces superheated steam.

## Question ID: 42
### Question: What is the term for vapor that exists above its boiling (saturation) temperature?

**Options:**
- a) Saturated vapor
- b) Superheated vapor
- c) Dry vapor
- d) Compressed vapor


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Superheated vapor is above saturation temperature. The degrees of superheat = actual T - saturation T.

## Question ID: 43
### Question: Heat that changes a substance's phase without changing its temperature is called:

**Options:**
- a) Sensible heat
- b) Specific heat
- c) Latent heat
- d) Internal heat


**Correct:** c
**Explanation:** Latent heat is phase-change heat (melting, vaporization) that doesn't change temperature. Sensible heat does change temperature.

---

## Question ID: 44
### Question: What term describes the fraction of vapor present in a wet steam mixture?

**Options:**
- a) Moisture content
- b) Steam quality
- c) Void fraction
- d) Dryness fraction


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Steam quality (X) = fraction of vapor in wet steam mixture. Moisture content (M) = liquid fraction. X + M = 100%.

## Question ID: 45
### Question: On a P-T diagram, the point at which the solid, liquid, and gas phases can all exist in equilibrium simultaneously is called:

**Options:**
- a) Critical Point
- b) Triple Point
- c) Saturation Point
- d) Equilibrium Point


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** The Triple Point is where solid, liquid, and gas coexist. For water: 0.01°C, 0.006 atm.

## Question ID: 46
### Question: According to Charles' Law, at constant pressure, the volume of a gas is:

**Options:**
- a) Inversely proportional to its absolute temperature
- b) Directly proportional to its absolute temperature
- c) Independent of temperature
- d) Proportional to the square of temperature


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Charles' Law: V ∝ T at constant P. V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ (using absolute temperature).

---

## Question ID: 47
### Question: According to Boyle's Law, at constant temperature, the volume of a gas is:

**Options:**
- a) Directly proportional to its absolute pressure
- b) Inversely proportional to its absolute pressure
- c) Independent of pressure
- d) Proportional to the square of pressure


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Boyle's Law: V ∝ 1/P at constant T. P₁V₁ = P₂V₂.

---

## Question ID: 48
### Question: Why should the Ideal Gas Law not be used to determine the properties of steam?

**Options:**
- a) Steam is always at high pressure
- b) Steam's behavior is considerably more complex than an ideal gas, so steam tables (experimentally derived data) must be used instead
- c) The Ideal Gas Law only works for perfect gases
- d) Steam has no molecules


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Steam deviates significantly from ideal gas behavior, especially near saturation. Steam tables provide experimentally accurate properties.

## Question ID: 94
### Question: What does the 'void fraction' represent in two-phase flow?

**Options:**
- a) The mass fraction of vapor
- b) The volumetric fraction of vapor (gas) in the mixture
- c) The quality of the steam
- d) The density difference

## Question ID: 95
### Question: On a P-T diagram, what is the significance of the Critical Point?

**Options:**
- a) The highest pressure at which solid can exist
- b) The highest temperature and pressure at which liquid and gas can exist as distinguishable phases
- c) The point where all phases coexist
- d) The freezing point of the substance


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** At the Critical Point, the distinction between liquid and gas phases disappears. Above the critical point, only a supercritical fluid exists.

## Question ID: 96
### Question: What are 'degrees of superheat' in a thermodynamic process?

**Options:**
- a) The absolute temperature of the vapor
- b) The difference between the actual temperature and the saturation temperature of the vapor
- c) The heat added to the system
- d) The pressure above saturation


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Degrees of superheat = T_actual - T_saturation. For example, if steam is at 350°F when saturation is 300°F, superheat = 50°F.

## Question ID: 97
### Question: What are 'degrees of subcooling' for a liquid?

**Options:**
- a) The absolute temperature of the liquid
- b) The difference between the saturation temperature and the actual (lower) temperature of the liquid
- c) The pressure below saturation
- d) The density of the liquid


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Degrees of subcooling = T_saturation - T_actual. Condensate at 100°F when saturation is 110°F has 10°F of subcooling.

## Question ID: 98
### Question: Why does cavitation occur in a pump?

**Options:**
- a) Excessive temperature in the system
- b) The liquid pressure drops below saturation pressure, causing vapor bubbles to form; they collapse later, causing erosion and damage
- c) Too much flow through the pump
- d) Worn pump bearings


**Correct:** b
**Explanation:** Cavitation: local pressure < saturation pressure → vapor bubbles form → downstream, pressure increases → bubbles collapse violently → erosion and vibration.
